# 23 — Referrer Trust: what support coordinators look for, and what the site must show

Date: 3 Nov 2026. Method: a research agent read coordinator-facing sources. Pages on ndis.gov.au and accc.gov.au returned HTTP 403 and ndiscommission.gov.au timed out, and nothing was worked around, so **NDIA, Commission and ACCC statements below are "Reported" (from search summaries), not read on the official page**. Verify each on the official site before relying on it.

## Verified (page read)

- **What coordinators say they look for** (MyPlanManager coordinator panels): consistent quality for every client; staff retention; being easy to contact for both coordinator and participant; listening to what the person wants; clear service agreements and cancellation/termination terms; honest capacity ("they ask for capacity directly"); recommendations from their own networks; they "try out providers twice" and keep their own database of trusted providers. Facebook coordinator network groups are an information source.
- **Regional reality** (La Trobe/Summer Foundation paper, Apr 2026): rural participants face limited choice and long waits; participants flag conflicts where the same organisation delivers support coordination and SIL/SDA; some were referred to unvetted services that seemed to rely on Google reviews; they want "trusted referrals or credible client feedback".
- **Directories**: MyCareSpace SIL/SDA listing is by application, needs evidence of SIL/SDA experience, prices unpublished (providersupport@mycarespace.com.au). My Plan Manager lists six directories: Clickability, Disability Support Guide, Karista, MyCareSpace, My Provider Directory, FindMyNDIS (most offer free listings). Housing Hub lists SDA/SIL housing and names coordinators as users (sign-up and subscription plans; prices not shown). Mable is a support-worker marketplace, a poor fit for SIL/SDA.
- **ACCC (secondary summary of its Feb 2026 report)**: most complaints concern false advertising, especially "NDIS approved"; unfair contract terms include high cancellation fees and long notice periods.

## Reported (not read on the official page)

- Coordinators must give transparent advice and disclose any interest in an option; guidance suggests offering at least three options. **Implication: because AblePoint offers support coordination alongside SIL/SDA, it needs a visible conflict-of-interest statement** (drafted on the new referrer page, owner to adopt or change).
- The NDIS Commission's public register is searchable by name, ABN and status and shows registration groups and conditions.
- **SIL mandatory registration (2026):** reportedly unregistered SIL providers had to apply by 1 October 2026 or stop; new SIL Practice Standards apply at next audit. Confirm on the Commission's mandatory-registration (SIL) page and state AblePoint's status plainly.
- Registered providers must have complaints and incident systems, NDIS worker screening clearances and quality audits.
- **Where referrers search:** NDIS Provider Finder (inside the myplace portal; providers choose whether to appear and whether to show an address; participants can filter "accepting new referrals"); SDA Finder (providers notify vacancies online; listings within about 5 days; weekly refresh; registered providers only); NDIA SDA vacancy-matching platforms (Housing Hub). No national official SIL vacancy register was found.
- **Testimonials and reviews:** no NDIS-specific rule found. Australian Consumer Law bans misleading conduct and fake testimonials; AHPRA bans testimonials about clinical aspects for regulated health-service advertising. Do not say "NDIS approved/endorsed/funded"; say "NDIS registered provider" with the registration groups. Inference (not sourced): get documented informed consent, do not identify residents or their homes, and never reward reviews.
- Hospital discharge: NDIA Hospital Interface Team and medium-term accommodation matter to discharge planners; no NSW Health-specific pathway found.

## Recommended coordinator-facing actions (beyond the website)

Monthly capacity and vacancy email to a consented list (A12); a named referral contact; fast, honest "we can / we cannot" answers; offer a short house visit; ask coordinators for feedback after each referral; attend regional coordinator network meetings; never pay or reward referrals (Code of Conduct, guardrail G-02).
